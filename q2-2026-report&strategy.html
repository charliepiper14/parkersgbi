How To Read This Deck

Three numbers, measured three different ways

Meta and Google count results differently, and both overlap with the store. To keep this honest we report each platform on its own basis and treat Shopify as the single source of truth for what actually happened.

Meta

Counts purchases

Meta reports purchases on a 7 day click and 1 day view window. In Q2 it recorded 1,504 purchases worth $240,330. This is the closest platform number to a real order.

Google

Counts conversions

Google counts conversions, which include some secondary actions, so the count runs high. Brand alone shows 715 conversions on $440, which is a measure of branded capture, not 715 separate orders.

Shopify

Counts reality

The store is the truth. Q2 delivered $616,222 gross sales and 2,092 new customers. Every efficiency claim in this deck is anchored back to these figures.

The one rule we hold to

We never add Meta and Google figures together, and we never compare their ROAS directly. Both platforms claim some of the same orders, and they count in different ways. Where we need a single efficiency number, we use total paid spend against real Shopify new customers.

Platform claimed revenue, Meta $240,330 plus Google $132,413, comes to $372,743. That is more than half of store gross sales, with overlap between the two, which is exactly why we reconcile rather than sum.

Reading it straight

  • We are spending $57.1K across Meta and Google and bringing in 2,092 new customers, which is roughly $27 each.
  • Against a lifetime value of $300 to $500, that is a return of eleven to eighteen times over a customer's life, far above the standard three to one health line.

Q1 Meta ran a single evergreen engine. Q2 added the World Cup campaign and the Francesca partnership, which scaled spend and volume but at lower efficiency.

Why efficiency compressed

  • World Cup campaign at 3.22x. It absorbed $8,623 below the 4.0x target, pulling the blended number down.

The honest read

None of this is a broken account. It is a quarter where we scaled into fatigue and a seasonal campaign. Q3 is about consolidating back into proven winners and refreshing creative, not adding budget.

Q3 Strategy · Meta

Consolidate into winners, refresh the creative, open the fish lever

The Meta plan is about discipline, not budget. We pull spend out of fatigue and the seasonal campaign, refresh the engine, and finally test a category the store is already selling.

Actions, in order

  • The World Cup campaign ends with the tournament, so we lift its one proven winner, the Missing Home carousel, into the evergreen now and switch off the World Cup LLA 1% straight away.
  • Refresh evergreen creative to fix the frequency creep on Value Optimisation, using fresh cuts from the ongoing video contract.
  • Scale the LLA audience. It returns the cheapest customers and is under-funded.
  • Launch fish creative. Smoked Kippers, a proper British fish shop angle and seaside nostalgia, into the Fish and Chips ad sets already built.
  • Judge Francesca on a clean 5 to 7 day read, then decide on the full 30 day window.

What this should achieve

  • Blended Meta ROAS back toward 4.5x to 5.0x by consolidating into proven performers.
  • Cost per purchase back toward $28 to $30.
  • A new winning category in fish, reducing reliance on the same pie and box creative.
  • A fresher creative rotation that protects the strongest audience from fatigue.
Proven themes we keep leaning on

Pies, build a box, bacon comparisons, founder and behind the scenes content, and customer testimonials. These are the backbone, and fish joins them.

Q3 Strategy · Google

Scale the proven Shopping engine, add a new acquisition channel

Google is in a strong position. The plan is to keep scaling what works, make a clear call on the rebuild, and open the first net new acquisition channel since Shopping went live.

Actions, in order

  • Keep scaling Shopping Feed Only in controlled 20% steps with 5 to 7 day spacing, inside the $3,000 monthly Google ceiling.
  • Make the Sales PMax v2 call at its next checkpoint. If it lags Shopping economics, fold its budget into Shopping.
  • Launch Demand Gen behind the new YouTube videos and the Winback Customer Match list, the first new acquisition channel since Shopping.
  • Complete the four Merchant Center fixes to reach Top Quality Store.
  • Expand non-brand reach. Load the agreed search themes and push Shopping and PMax into the demand we are not yet capturing.

What this should achieve

  • Shopping held above 10x value per cost while it takes more budget.
  • A clean decision on PMax v2 rather than letting it drift.
  • A genuine top of funnel channel in Demand Gen, feeding the rest of the account.
  • More non-brand customers, reducing reliance on people who already know the name.
Budget note

Google ran at 96% of the $3,000 ceiling in the back half of Q2. Scaling Shopping further may warrant a modest lift to that ceiling, which we will quantify before any change.

Where the next dollars go

  • Meta, Phase 1. As the World Cup ends, redirect its spend into LLA, a fish test at around $50 to $75 a day, and an evergreen refresh. No net increase yet.
  • Meta, Phases 2 and 3. Scale LLA and proven winners in 20% steps once ROAS is back above 4.5x.
  • Google. Lift the $3,000 ceiling in 20% steps, all into Shopping first, then Demand Gen at around $25 a day in Phase 2.

The scaling rules we hold to

  • 20% maximum per step, 5 to 7 day spacing, to respect the learning cycle.
  • Cost per new customer stays inside $25 to $35 at every level.
  • Any campaign below its gate pauses its next step until it recovers.
  • Reallocation before expansion, so the mix is fixed before budget is added.
Net effect by the end of Q3

Paid media moves from around $26,500 to around $37,200 a month by the end of Q3, roughly 40% higher, with cost per new customer held in target. Early phases are funded by reallocation, later phases by proven efficiency.
